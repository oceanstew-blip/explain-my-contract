SCORING METHOD
======================================================================

The supplied answer key awards 0-2 points for each of eight dimensions:
1. Accurate parties and term
2. Accurate price
3. Accurate deliverables
4. Accurate deadlines
5. Accurate renewal and exit terms
6. Accurate restrictions and obligations
7. Accurate privacy and data terms
8. Fair provisions and protections surfaced

Maximum: 16 points per run.

The supplied deductions were applied as written:
- Minus 2 for an invented clause.
- Minus 2 for an invented legal conclusion.
- Minus 2 for falsely claiming a simulated signature is real.

Target: at least 14/16 per document.

Scoring notes:
- Information may appear in the snapshot, findings, or protections to receive
  credit. It does not have to appear in every section.
- A point was removed when a material benchmark item was omitted or materially
  weakened.
- The false-signature deduction was applied when a report said "your signature"
  committed or bound the reader. The source documents state that signatures
  are simulated, so this is a factual error even though the test's selected
  user intent was "already signed."
- No legal-enforceability conclusion was credited or inferred by this review.

======================================================================
CROSS-RUN QUALITY FINDINGS
======================================================================

What worked consistently:
1. Parties, agreement type, term, prices, and core deliverables were highly
   accurate across the pack.
2. The system differentiated balanced, mixed, and deliberately adverse
   contracts instead of producing identical generic warnings.
3. Adverse monetary terms were reliably surfaced: acceleration, unauthorized
   hourly billing, export fees, administrative fees, renewal prices, and late
   charges.
4. Deadline extraction was strong: claims, appeals, review periods, renewal
   notices, cure periods, termination notices, return windows, and response
   deadlines were usually exact.
5. Balanced-contract protections were generally visible, especially refunds,
   written-change controls, milestone gates, warranty/insurance, audit limits,
   honest-review rights, and ownership of custom work.
6. Action steps were usually concrete: calendar a date, preserve proof, request
   itemization, test a link, back up data, or use the specified notice channel.
7. Structured-output validation held: every successful run returned a usable
   report object with the expected snapshot, categories, findings, protections,
   and informational notice.

What must improve:
1. Simulated signature grounding — highest priority
   Nine of 20 reports falsely used "your signature" language. The application
   must distinguish the user's intent selection from evidence in the document.
   If the file says signatures are simulated, the report must not state or imply
   that a real person signed it.

2. Repeat-run completeness
   Pass 2 omitted the no-auto-renewal protection in Coaching Fair One and the
   exclusive-workflow-ownership risk in CRM Implementation Adverse. A report
   should have a required-clause completeness check before acceptance, especially
   for ownership, renewal, termination, price, and data access.

3. Severity calibration
   Routine, transparent terms were sometimes marked high attention merely
   because they created payment obligations. High attention should be reserved
   for unusually costly, one-sided, hard-to-reverse, urgent, or safety/privacy
   significant terms. Balanced installments and capped termination payments
   generally belong at important or informational unless context makes them
   unusually dangerous.

4. Consequence wording
   Some "danger" fields converted a contractual process into a categorical legal
   result, for example saying a party "cannot" recover or "will" lose a right.
   Safer, more accurate wording is "the contract states," "may limit," or "the
   agreement does not provide," unless a qualified legal determination exists.

5. Low-value drafting observations
   Party-label switching was sometimes elevated into a formal finding. Such a
   note can be useful, but it should not crowd out ownership, renewal, privacy,
   payment, or termination issues and should be clearly ranked below material
   business risks.

VIABILITY DECISION AND REQUIRED EXIT CRITERIA
======================================================================

Initial decision before remediation: technically viable; content quality was
not yet cleared for launch.

Exit criteria used for the same-night remediation:

1. Fix signature-state handling.
   - Detect explicit "simulated," "sample," "fictional," or "not executed"
     language in extracted text.
   - Never claim a signature is real solely because the user selected
     "already signed."
   - Prefer "the contract states" over "your signature commits you" unless
     execution is actually established.

2. Add deterministic critical-clause coverage checks.
   - Require explicit review of price, term, renewal, termination, deadlines,
     ownership, data access/export, confidentiality/privacy, dispute process,
     liability/indemnity, and material protections.
   - Reject or repair a structured response when a source contains a critical
     clause that is absent from all report sections.

3. Tighten attention-level rules.
   - High attention: severe financial exposure, one-sided control, short trap
     deadlines, broad indemnity/disclaimer, costly lock-in, ownership/data loss,
     or other material asymmetric risk.
   - Important: operational obligations, ordinary deadlines, and balanced
     negotiated constraints.
   - Document quality: drafting ambiguity only when it could materially change
     who owes what or make performance unclear.

4. Re-run this exact pack twice after fixes.
   - Required result: 20/20 reports retrieved.
   - Required answer-key score: every run at least 14/16.
   - Required grounding result: zero false signature claims.
   - Required critical-clause result: zero omission of renewal, termination,
     ownership, data access/export, price, or major protections.
   - Recommended performance result: document the provider path and keep p95
     user-visible completion time within the product's stated expectation.

5. Complete separate payment/access verification before any launch.
   - This run intentionally did not test or complete Stripe checkout.
   - Test both configured prices and the beta promotion only in test mode.
   - Verify signature-checked webhooks, server-controlled price/access,
     idempotency, and fail-closed report access.
   - Do not enable live payments without explicit approval.

6. Complete production build and deployment review separately.
   - This run did not deploy or merge.
   - Current analysis improvements remain uncommitted in the local worktree.
   - Run the full check/build suite again after any content-quality fixes.

SAME-NIGHT FIX AND CONTROLLED RETEST — SEPTEMBER 22, 2026
======================================================================

The launch-blocking findings above were addressed locally and the same ten
fictional contracts were run through the live analysis path again.

Implemented corrections:
1. Execution-state grounding
   - The prompt now states that the selected "already signed" workflow is not
     proof that a real signature exists.
   - It prohibits "your signature commits," "your signature means," "your
     signature binds," and equivalent claims.
   - The server also detects source text stating that a document or signature
     is fictional, synthetic, sample, simulated, unsigned, or not executed and
     neutralizes false execution claims before storage.
   - Legitimate clause wording such as "$4,500 due at signature" is preserved.

2. Critical-clause completeness
   - The prompt now requires a final source-to-report audit covering price,
     extra charges, deliverables, deadlines, renewal, termination, refunds,
     ownership/licenses, data access/export/deletion/retention,
     confidentiality/publicity, liability/indemnity, and disputes.
   - A critical term present in the source must appear in the snapshot,
     findings, or protections.

3. Severity calibration
   - High attention is now reserved for unusually substantial financial
     consequences, hard-to-stop payments, lock-in, broad indemnity, loss of
     ownership/data access, short trap deadlines, or significant liability.
   - Clear ordinary installment schedules, milestone payments, mutual
     short-notice exits, capped refund deductions, and balanced change-order
     processes are not high attention merely because they create obligations.

4. Fallback structured-output recovery
   - The first controlled retest exposed a 502 when Gemini exhausted its model
     sequence and OpenAI returned a schema-invalid result.
   - The OpenAI fallback now retries schema-invalid structured output within a
     bounded loop.
   - A clean batch then completed 19 of 20 first requests. The twentieth request
     returned two consecutive schema-invalid OpenAI outputs and failed closed.
   - A targeted third attempt for that same fictional equipment lease succeeded
     and produced a complete private report.
   - The bounded fallback repair limit was therefore raised to three attempts,
     which matches the demonstrated recovery point while remaining fail-closed
     after repeated invalid output.

Retest operational evidence:
